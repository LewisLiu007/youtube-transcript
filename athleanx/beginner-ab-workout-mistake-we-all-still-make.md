# Beginner Ab Workout Mistake (WE ALL STILL MAKE!)

Stop doing marathon ab workouts and start getting results here…

What's up, guys? Jeff Cavaliere, AthleanX. com. Today I want to cover the biggest beginner
ab training mistakes that even advanced lifters continue to make. That is: the number of reps that we're doing,
or focusing on the number of reps that we're doing in our ab workouts.

You see, even when people say to me that they've
done 1000 rep ab workouts and they do them every day I'm not impressed. Not at all because the number of reps that
you do doesn't give me any indication about how much intensity you brought to that ab
workout. As a matter of fact, most often, unless you're
a really advanced trainee, the number of reps – the higher they go – the lower your
intensity is. If you want your abs to show you'd better
start flipping that equation around. Start focusing more on a high quality contraction
of your abs and I promise you, with far fewer numbers and far less time spent; you'll see
much better result from your ab training.

Think of it this way: if you're going to be
doing this ab scissor here and you focus only on how long you can go and how long your legs
can stay off the ground and go, what's most likely going to happen is your legs will get
tired. You'll feel it in your hip flexors, but you
shouldn't ever have that happen to you because if you're doing it right you're not focusing
on your hip flexors, but you're shifting that focus to your abs. You intensify the contraction by lifting your
trunk up off the ground like I'm doing here. My shoulders are off the ground, I'm getting
that top-down crunch and I'm activating my abs form the top down. The purpose of the legs is to really act as
a disturbance to my ability to hold this position.

It's that back and forth, rocking and shaking
of my torso from the bottom up that's challenging me to be able to hold here, but it's not to
tire out my hip flexors. If I do it right and I intensify that contraction
in the abs this exercise becomes a whole hell of a lot harder. Who cares if I only last 30 seconds, versus
being able to do it for 3 minutes the other way? The 30 seconds was actually targeted at the
muscles we're trying to develop. Overload is everything.

Another equivalent is trying to do bicep curls
with a 5lb dumbbell. Am I doing a curl? Yes. Am I executing a movement called a "curl"? Yes.

Am I doing anything to develop my biceps? No. That's the same concept here that I just tried
to cover with the abs. Try to focus on causing enough of an overload
by increasing the intensity and forget all about counting your ab reps. Forget all about how long you're doing them.

the limits. I promise you it will. Was it damn hard? Yes, it was. Could I have done it for an hour?

Absolutely not. Did it get the job done? 100%. So start focusing less on numbers, time, reps,
and everything else; just focus more on the quality of your contractions no matter what
ab exercise you do. There you go!
