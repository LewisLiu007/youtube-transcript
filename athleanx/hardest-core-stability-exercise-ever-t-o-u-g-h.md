# Hardest Core Stability Exercise Ever (T-O-U-G-H!!)

Build a ripped core and athletic muscle from head to toe here…

What's up, guys? Jeff Cavalier, Athlean-X. com. Let me cut right to the chase. If you can
do this core movement, then I am applauding you right now.

This is one of the toughest
core exercises you can do. All it requires is a bench, and a couple kettle
bells to get it done. Here's how you do it: you put yourself up here as I'm doing. Feet
on the bench, kettle bells out in front of me right about shoulder level. You grab onto the kettle bells.

Now you try
to perform a renegade row. You might have done renegade rows before and figured they're
not all that hard. Of course they're not because you have the
leverage and the benefit of having two feet firmly on the ground, allowing you to push
through your toes to initiate that lift off of the kettle bell form the ground. Not so when you elevate your feet. Immediately
you'll find that you're at a big disadvantage which means you're at a big advantage to getting
your core to work a lot harder.

So as you can see, I try to alternate pulling
left and right while maintaining rotational stability of my spine. I don’t want to allow
my trunk to rotate in any one direction. First of all, I could fall off of the kettle bells
here, or fall of the bench. We don’t want that. I'm also getting the
benefit of tying in on this closed chain movement.

My shoulder girdle with my core – because
after all, your shoulder girdle is a core muscle group at the end of the day. You want to make sure that these muscles can
work together, especially if you have athletic aspirations of becoming a lot more powerful
and strong starting with your core. Now, don’t worry if you can't do this. This is certainly not an easy core exercise. I would have called it "easy core exercise" if I meant it.

It's not. It's difficult, but
you can start with a little bit of a regression. Even in this elevated position, without having
to raise the dumbbells or the kettle bells off the ground you just simply do an arm lift
and try to alternate your arm lifts left and right. You are in a three point flank position here
so it becomes pretty difficult. It's a good starting spot for somebody that can't necessarily
do this renegade row version just yet.
